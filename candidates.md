# Candidates and duel record — Tell a Whole Story in 32 Characters

**Entry shipped:** `MyDogIsFamous_ImStaff` — 21 characters, 21 bytes.
Gate: UTF-8 text, 1–32 characters, not only white space. ✓

**Judge brief (published in the kit, z-ai/glm-5.3-flash, deadpan comedy
editor), criteria in order:**
1. A Wi-Fi name ≤32 chars that reads as a joke and hints at a story
   (a plain label or slogan loses).
2. The joke lands on the first read, with a clear twist or punchline a
   stranger gets without explanation.
3. It tells a whole story: a situation, a character and something that
   happened, so a reader can fill in the rest.
4. It is fresh: avoids the stock Wi-Fi jokes, or turns one into
   something new.
5. It is tight: every character earns its place, clean, readable, easy
   to say out loud. Shock value for its own sake does not count.

Every duel below was played in both orders (A/B swapped), scored by the
brief's criteria in order; a game is the mean of its two calls, exactly
as the house scores it.

## Field scan

The live field was sampled first-hand through the submissions API
(artifact texts are public once graded): 488 artifact texts fetched in
the first pass (437 unique), then the remaining 974 for a full pass —
**1,462 texts, 1,216 unique**. The field is enormous (~1,500
submissions at scan time) and brutally concentrated:

- **Divorce / ex-took-the-dog-router-WiFi / password-changed lanes:
  saturated** — 100+ variants (`MyExTookTheRouterAndTheDog` ×6,
  `Ex took the dog. I kept the WiFi` ×3, `SheGotTheHouseIGotTheWiFi`
  ×3, `MyExChangedThePasswordAgain` ×3, …). Any draft in this family
  starts with a freshness penalty.
- Also busy: ex still pays/uses this WiFi; dad went for milk / dad
  left; password died with grandma/grandpa; mom/dad found the search
  history; dog ate the router/homework; WiFi died/outlived the
  marriage; FBI van variants; proposals made via Wi-Fi name
  (`He proposed. I said buffering` — which killed draft D7 below);
  WiFi-down-so-the-family-talked.
- **Stock names present verbatim:** `It Hurts When IP`,
  `Pretty Fly for a Wi-Fi`, `The LAN Before Time` — criterion 4 bait.
- **Empty or near-empty lanes:** pet fame/influencer pets, haunted
  house economics, prenups, twins, gamer grandparents' rivals aside —
  and the full pass confirms it: across all 1,462 graded texts there
  are **zero** containing "Famous", "Staff", "Influencer",
  "Follower", "Prenup", "Haunted", "Tinder", "Elvis", "Gnome" or
  "Snore". (The ghost-rent lane does have occupants — `Ghost Pays
  Half the WiFi`, `Ghost roommate still pays rent` — which mattered
  in the round-2 playoff below.)

## Champion vs the field's strongest (both orders)

D12's trial exposed the flaw the winners study warns about: a champion
that only beat its own drafts. Against the sparring set D12 went:
**L** vs F1 (funeral-Zoom stakes outgun whimsy, margins 1–2),
**L** vs F5 ("Next?" is a perfect one-word twist),
**L** vs F4 — the decisive loss: `GrandmaClicked_WeLostTheHouse` is
the *same* grandparent-tech-disaster family with catastrophic stakes,
so D12 is neither fresher nor funnier in its own lane,
**level** vs F2, **level** vs F8 (D12 fresher, F8 the more classic
irony). ≈1.0 of 5 games — well below the ≥0.70 bar. **Iterate.**

**Final champion: I1 `MyDogIsFamous_ImStaff` (21 chars).**

Why it can travel: it is a two-beat joke a stranger gets in one read;
it implies a whole modern story (the dog has followers, sponsors,
a content schedule — and a human whose job is now filming him); it
plays a lane the ~1,500-entry field left empty, so there is no
byte-identical merge and no freshness collision; and at 21 bytes it
is among the tightest story-bearing names in the field, which is
also the ranking tie-break after win rate.
